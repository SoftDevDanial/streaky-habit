## What changed from F
Two things, both from the owner's note of 10 October.

1. **P4, the overview's year view.** The overview still shows the last 12 weeks. A Year button on the overview opens P4, every habit together over the last 12 months (Friday 10 October 2025 to Saturday 10 October 2026, the same 366 days as P2). Each day is the overview's own small ring, one segment per habit and a filled core when all are done, laid out month by month with the newest month first, as P2 is. Tapping a day opens P1 for that day. On a desktop the Year button sits on the band's title line and P4 is a centred dialog with three months across. On a phone it sits in P3's header beside Close and P4 is a full-screen sheet over P3, one month across.
2. **A bin on every habit row.** It opens M1 for that habit. Delete habit in P2 stays.

Nothing else moved. Tokens, type, radii, motion, the sample data and every figure, the add field, the no-scroll page and the Prototype states tab are F's. P4 is drawn by F's own `ring()` and `dayCell()` inside P2's month layout (now one shared `monthsHTML()`), and it reads the same `habits` sets as everything else, so a day reads the same in the overview, P2 and P4 and a tick anywhere redraws every view and every Current and Best at once.

## Where the bin sits on a row, and why
| Width | Layout | Bin to Done today |
|---|---|---|
| 360 and 390 | Two lines as in F. The bin is at the right end of the **name line**. Current, Best, Year and Done today stay on the second line exactly as F had them | 12 px of clear space between the two targets, the bin above and Done today below |
| 1280 | One line. Name, then the bin in its own 56 px column, then Current and Best, Year, Done today | About 300 px, with the numbers and the Year button in between |

The reasons, in order of weight.
- **It fits at 360.** F's second line uses about 300 of the 310 px a row has at 360. A 44 px bin does not fit there without squeezing Done today or wrapping the numbers. The name line has the room.
- **It keeps the bin apart from Done today.** Done today is the daily tap and stays where F put it, at the right of the numbers line under the thumb. The bin is on a different line on a phone and on the far side of the numbers on a desktop. A tap aimed at Done today has to miss by 12 px upward on a phone, and on a desktop the two never share a neighbourhood.
- **It says what it acts on.** Delete is about the habit, so it sits with the name. Done today and Year are about its days, so they sit with its numbers. The same rule gives both layouts.
- **Order matches sight.** The bin comes first in the row's tab order on both layouts (bin, Year, Done today), which is also the reading order on screen.

The cost is height on a phone. The bin's 44 px target and a 12 px gap make a phone row 114 px tall instead of F's 98. Long names wrap on the name line beside the bin (checked with a 40 character name at 360, 390 and 1280).

## How each inventory row is reached
| ID | Control |
|---|---|
| K1 | The default view on load. Also "K1 Key screen" in Prototype states |
| P1 | Tap any day up to today in the overview band (desktop), in the Overview popup P3 (phone) or in P4 (both) |
| P2 | The Year button on any row (accessible name "Year view, Read 20 pages") |
| P3 | The Overview button in the phone header (below 1024 px wide) |
| **P4** | **Desktop**, the Year button on the overview band's title line. **Phone**, the Year button in P3's header, beside Close. Both are named "Year view, all habits" |
| **M1** | **The bin on any row** (named `Delete "Read 20 pages"`), **or** Delete habit inside P2 |
| S1 to S5 | The Prototype states tab, top right of the header on a desktop and on its own line above the header on a phone |

Esc closes the top layer first. On a phone that is P1, then P4, then P3, each Esc closing one (checked at 390 and 360). On a desktop it is P1, then P4. The backdrop closes P1, P2, P4 and M1 on a desktop. Close buttons close P1, P2, P3 and P4. P4's Close on a phone returns to P3 with focus on P3's Year button.

## Readings I made
These continue F's list (1 to 19 in `f.md`).

20. **P4's ring size on a desktop.** The overview's 40 px ring keeps its 16 px day number. In a 42 px column that is 294 px per month, so three months fit across P2's own 980 px dialog with 20 px between them. The months grid fills by width, so 1024 px also gets three months and a tablet gets two. Rings have about 8 px of air between them, the band has more, which is the price of three across.
21. **P4 on a phone** uses P3's touch sizes, a 42 px ring in a 48 px tall cell at 390 (16.8 px numbers) and a 40 px ring in a 46 px cell at 360 (16 px numbers). Every day is at least 46 px each way (48 by 46 at 360, 51 by 48 at 390).
22. **Closed and blank days in P4** follow P2. The 11th to the 31st of October 2026 show their number dim with no ring and cannot be tapped. The 1st to the 9th of October 2025 are empty cells.
23. **P4's day names carry the year** ("Tue 22 Sep 2026, 1 of 3 habits"), because P4 holds two Octobers. The band and P3 keep F's shorter names.
24. **P4's header** follows P2's. Title, subtitle and Close on top, then the hint on the left and the None to All legend on the right. There is no Delete or Current and Best, as P4 is not one habit.
25. **The band's Year button** sits on the title line of the band's 180 px left column, right aligned, and the title stays where F had it. The hint moves down 8 px. The band does not grow (still 354 px), so the list frame keeps F's 210 px at 1280 by 800.
26. **P3's Year button** sits between the title and Close, top aligned with Close.
27. **P1 over P4.** On a phone it is the bottom sheet over P4, and P4 scrolls so the chosen day stays in sight above it with its amber outline (F reading 15, now for P4 too). On a desktop it is the centred dialog over P4.
28. **Focus around P4.** P4 opens with focus on Close, as P2 and P3 do. Closing P4 returns focus to the Year button that opened it.
29. **Focus after a delete depends on where it started.** From a row it goes to the next row's bin, the previous row's when the last row went, or the add field when the list is empty, as the brief says. From P2 it keeps F reading 18 and goes to the next row's Year button, since that is where the person came from. Cancel, Esc or the backdrop return focus to the bin that opened M1.
30. **The bin's look.** A 44 px circle like the other icon-only buttons (Close, Dismiss), per the radius rule. Grey icon at rest. On hover the icon and a thin ring turn the danger colour. On keyboard focus the icon turns the danger colour inside the white focus ring. Never a fill. The focus ring is drawn inside the circle because the first row's bin sits close to the frame's top edge, where an outer ring would be clipped by the frame.
31. **The bin in S3.** With 12 habits each row has its bin, and a delete from a row enables the field again at once. "Delete one to add another." stays as written.
32. **Prototype states hint** now names P4 and the row bin. It is review tooling only.

## What it costs
- A phone row is 16 px taller (114 against 98). K1 at 390 by 844 still shows the three habits with room. S3 at 360 by 780 shows four rows and a sliver of the fifth before the frame scrolls, where F showed four and most of a fifth.
- On a desktop the bin sits closer to the numbers than to a short name, so it reads as part of the row's tools more than as part of the name. It does fill some of the empty middle F's notes called out.
- P4 adds 366 more tab stops, and it redraws its 366 rings on every tick while it is open.
- Two buttons now read "Year". Their accessible names differ (one habit or all habits) and their places differ (on a row or on the overview).
- With twelve habits each P4 ring is twelve short segments, which reads as a dotted circle. That is D's rule and the overview already does it.
