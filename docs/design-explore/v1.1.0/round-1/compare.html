Streaky Habit v1.1.0, design round 1

Five directions for the key screen, each drawn by its own designer from the same brief. Every frame below is the real prototype, so you can tick days, open a past day, press a bin, and use Prototype states inside it for the other states. The desktop view is further down.

My pick, A Night Console

It is the closest to your words "like discord". Every past day in its 12-week log shows its date and is a full-size button, which serves the day editor, the feature this version exists for. Ticking a day turns an open circle into a filled teal tile with a check, the most rewarding tap of the five. One teal on layered slate keeps it minimal, and the desktop gets a real two-pane layout.

Runner-up B, Neon Thread, if you want to keep the 1.0 idea of a thread joining done days, which also matches the logo. Its past days show no dates, so I would add them. A mix, for example A's dated log with B's lit thread on the strip, becomes one new direction in round 2.

Two decisions for you

1. Which direction. Pick one, ask for a mix (it becomes one new direction), or reject them all (round 2 brings new bets, not polished copies).

2. The 12-week history turns on its side. All five make weeks the rows and weekdays the 7 columns (C only on a phone). The 1.0 rule was 12 columns, one per week. The reason is the day editor. Every past day must now be a tap target, and 12 columns give about 24 px per day on a phone where 7 columns give 41 to 47 px. It changes a 1.0 rule, so it needs your OK. I recommend accepting it.

Direction A, my pick

Night Console

A chat app's dark console. Habits read like channels, Current is a live badge, and the 12 weeks are a scrollback log.

Bets on
Layered dark panes and one teal, the look you named. A done day morphs from an open circle into a filled tile with a check. Current is solid when today is done, dashed while the run still holds, grey at zero. Every past day prints its date.
Costs
The log sits below the habits on a phone. It is the most "app chrome" of the five, and desktop leaves space between the rows and the composer.
1.0 rules it changes
The 12 weeks turn on their side. Heat levels show as a growing bar as well as a tint.

Direction B, runner-up

Neon Thread

The 1.0 thread grows a light. Done days are lit nodes joined by a glowing trail, and the past is a quiet starfield.

Bets on
Glow spent in one place, the done nodes and the trail. The streak line is a real line, Current on the left and Best on the right. The day editor shows each habit's streak move as you tick.
Costs
Five lit nodes in a row is a lot of light. The starfield prints no dates. Two desktop layouts to build. Dark theme only.
1.0 rules it changes
The 12 weeks turn on their side. Heat levels are star size and tone.

Prototype only. The Prototype states tab covers the New habit field on a phone.

Direction C

Daylight Instrument

Futuristic in daylight. Oversized thin numerals, Current in cobalt and Best in ink, and past days as gauges.

Bets on
The streak is the number, so the number is huge. Past days are square gauges that fill a quarter, a half, three quarters or all the way.
Costs
The numerals take height, so a phone shows two and a half habits. A light page is the furthest from "like discord".
1.0 rules it changes
The 12 weeks turn on their side below 720 px only. Desktop keeps the 1.0 shape.

Prototype only. The Prototype states button floats over the strip on a phone and over the heatmap corner on desktop.

Direction D

Orbit Rings

Each habit is a ring of its last seven days, with Current in the centre and Best beside it.

Bets on
The ring is both the week and the control, and a run is a lit amber arc. The 12 weeks are small rings with one segment per habit. Desktop tiles the rings two by two.
Costs
Each card is tall. Ticks sit on a curve, which takes a moment to learn. Newest week on top, unlike the others.
1.0 rules it changes
The 12 weeks turn on their side, newest on top. The strip becomes a ring. Best shows "days".

Prototype only. The Prototype states tab covers part of the New habit field on a phone.

Direction E

Command Line

Keyboard first and monospaced. Habits are aligned rows of marks, and the add field is a command palette.

Bets on
Orange means live and nothing else. Keys are visible but never required. The calendar prints every date, and a day's level is a count of segments.
Costs
The calendar is tall on a phone. Monospace wraps long names sooner. Calm and precise rather than futuristic in the neon sense.
1.0 rules it changes
The 12 weeks turn on their side. One shared week header. Done is an ink square with a check.

Prototype only. The Prototype states tab covers some calendar days while you scroll on a phone.
