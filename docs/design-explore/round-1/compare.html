Streaks, design round 1

Three directions for the key screen, drawn independently from the same brief. Each frame is the phone view at 390 x 844 and scrolls. Open one full size to see the desktop layout.

Recommendation

B, Quiet beads. The brief asks for steady, calm and rewarding, and B is the only direction where the streak itself is drawn rather than counted: done days are joined by a thread, so a run is visible at a glance, and a dashed thread runs from yesterday into today's open bead, which shows the rule that a streak holds until today is over without a word of copy. A is a strong idea, but a cross still half reads as wrong and the boxed grid is busy. C makes the number the hero, which suits a sprint more than a routine, and its tall tiles push the add field and heatmap below the fold. One fix before locking B: heatmap levels 1 and 2 are too close, so they must differ more in size and tone.

Direction A

Wall calendar

Don't break the chain, wall-calendar style.

Bets on
The familiar chain of crossed-off days. Green crosses and tinted boxes join a run into one band.
Costs
A cross can still read as wrong. The boxed grid adds noise and each habit takes about 220 px on a phone.

Direction B · recommended

Quiet beads

Calm progress, no guilt.

Bets on
Done days are filled beads joined by a thread, so a streak is a visible line. A dashed thread into today shows the streak still holds.
Costs
A long streak may feel quiet. Heatmap levels 1 and 2 are close in tone.

Direction C

Scoreboard

The streak number is the hero.

Bets on
A big split-flap number per habit as the reward, a 7-segment bar beneath, an LED-matrix heatmap.
Costs
Loud numbers overpower the ticking. Tall tiles push the add field and heatmap below the first screen.
